## 1. Quantum Threat Model and Baseline Work Factors (Conjectured)

The post-quantum analysis of ITB separates into two distinct regimes corresponding to the construction's two security layers:

1. **Passive observation (information-theoretic noise absorption).** Under ciphertext-only observation, the Pixel Barrier interposes a fresh CSPRNG-seeded container generated by `internal/drbg` before embedding. Each wire byte retains 1 uniform noise bit at secret position `noisePos ∈ {0, ..., 7}` alongside 7 rotated and XOR-masked data bits. By [Proof 1](PROOFS.md#proof-1-information-theoretic-barrier) (`proof1_encode_bijection_left_inv`, `proof1_encode_bijection_right_inv`, and `proof1_unique_plaintext_for_observation` in `lean/ITB/PixelBarrier.lean`), across an unknown uniform data distribution under ciphertext-only attack (COA), the conditional distribution of every observed byte `v ∈ {0, ..., 255}` satisfies `P(C'[p,ch] = v | h) = 1/256 = P(C'[p,ch] = v)`, yielding zero mutual information `I(H(K); C') = 0` between the hash configuration and the observed wire byte. By the **Holevo bound** (Holevo 1973), for any ensemble of quantum states `{p_h, ρ_h}` encoding `C'`, the accessible classical information extractable by any positive operator-valued measure (POVM) is bounded above by the Holevo quantity `I_acc ≤ χ := S(ρ) − ∑_h p_h S(ρ_h)`. Because `ρ_h = ρ` is identical for every hash configuration `h`, `χ = 0` identically, so an unbounded fault-tolerant quantum computer gains no passive distinguishing advantage over a classical observer.

2. **Active seed recovery (computational and PRF-conditional).** Under known-plaintext (KPA), chosen-plaintext (CPA), or chosen-ciphertext (CCA) models where an attacker searches the seed space against a verification predicate, security is computational, conditional on the underlying PRF, and subject to quadratic Grover speedup over the effective seed search space.

### 1.1 Conservative Grover Baseline

In accordance with [SCIENCE.md § 3.3](SCIENCE.md#33-quantum-resistance-conjectured) and [SECURITY.md § 3](SECURITY.md#3-attack-resistance-summary), the baseline quantum work factors use the conservative Grover seed-search model across the three composition modes:

- **Core ITB (No MAC) and MAC + Silent Drop (with insider knowledge of the internal MAC tag or under Full KPA):** Without an external CCA oracle, `noiseSeed` and `dataSeed` are tightly coupled ([Proof 3](PROOFS.md#proof-3-8-seed-isolation)): without `dataSeed`, the output controlled by `noiseSeed` is indistinguishable from random container bits, forcing a joint search over `2^(2·keyBits)` seed pairs alongside `P` per-region `startPixel` candidates (`startSeed`). The classical brute-force cost is `P × 2^(2·keyBits)`, and the conservative Grover work factor is `√P × 2^keyBits` (~2^1028 at 1024-bit keys for **Mode 2** P = 441 and theoretical floor P = 400; ~2^1029 at **Mode 3** P = 784 and **Mode 1** P = 1225). Each Grover oracle call requires `O(P)` reversible circuit gates to evaluate the full container decryption and verification check. Under ciphertext-only observation in Core ITB (No MAC), or in MAC + Silent Drop when the attacker lacks insider knowledge of the internal MAC presence, no boolean verification predicate exists to mark the true key (see § 4).
- **MAC + Reveal (active CCA oracle):** When a deployment exposes MAC accept/reject responses to chosen bit flips, an active CCA attacker can locate the 1 noise bit per byte in 4.375 expected queries per pixel (~5359 queries across `P = 1225` pixels vs. `8P = 9800` exhaustive, [Proof 6](PROOFS.md#proof-6-cca-leak-upper-bound), `earlyStopQueries_sum_eq_35` and `earlyStopQueries_mode1_budget` in `lean/ITB/PixelBarrier.lean`). This leaks 3 bits per pixel (4.8 % of the 62-bit per-pixel configuration) and eliminates `noiseSeed` from the brute-force loop, while leaving `dataSeed`, `lockSeed`, and `startSeed` isolated ([Proof 3a](PROOFS.md#proof-3a-8-seed-isolation-minimality)) and preserving guaranteed encrypted DRBG residue in data bit positions ([Proof 10](PROOFS.md#proof-10-guaranteed-drbg-residue-no-perfect-fill)). The classical brute-force cost reduces from `P × 2^(2·keyBits)` to `P × 2^keyBits`, giving a conservative Grover work factor of `√P × 2^(keyBits/2)` (~2^516 at 1024-bit keys for **Mode 2** P = 441 and P = 400; ~2^517 at **Mode 3** P = 784 and **Mode 1** P = 1225), with `O(P)` decryption work per query.

| Composition Mode | Classical Seed Search | Conservative Grover Work Factor | 1024-bit Key (**Mode 2**, P = 441 / Floor P = 400) | 1024-bit Key (**Mode 1**, P = 1225 / **Mode 3**, P = 784) | Per-Query Oracle Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core ITB (No MAC) | P × 2^(2·keyBits) | √P × 2^keyBits (Full KPA; no COA oracle) | ~2^1028 | ~2^1029 | O(P) full-container decrypt (Full KPA); no COA oracle (§ 4) |

Under COA without insider knowledge, MAC + Silent Drop exposes no verification predicate and matches Core ITB.

**[2]** Assumes the attacker first completes the classical active CCA noise-bit discovery phase (`4.375 × P` sequential network queries) to eliminate `noiseSeed` before running offline quantum search over `dataSeed`.

### 1.2 Q1 vs. Q2 Quantum Attack Models

Cryptographic post-quantum threat models distinguish between **Q1** (the adversary collects classical encryption/decryption queries and performs offline quantum computation) and **Q2** (the adversary queries a remote encryption or decryption hardware box in coherent quantum superposition `∑_x α_x |x⟩|0⟩ → ∑_x α_x |x⟩|E_K(x)⟩`).

In ITB, Q2 superposition queries are structurally constrained by two design properties:

1. **Dual internal CSPRNG nonces per encryption.** Every call to `Encrypt3x{128,256,512}Cfg`, `EncryptAuthenticated3x{128,256,512}Cfg`, or `triple.Pipeline.Encrypt*` draws two independent nonces internally from `crypto/rand` (default `DefaultNonceBits = 512` bits each): a public `main_nonce` `N_m` on the wire header and a secret `interlock_nonce` `N_il` split across the three lanes under the Pixel Barrier ([PROOFS.md § Nonce Uniqueness](PROOFS.md#nonce-uniqueness)). Because the caller cannot supply or fix the nonces, each encryption invocation is parameterized by fresh entropy external to the attacker's query register, collapsing any coherent superposition over chosen plaintexts into a mixed density matrix across independent per-message configurations.
2. **Classical MAC verification boundary.** Under MAC + Reveal, the decryption oracle is a remote classical endpoint that measures the incoming ciphertext and returns a 1-bit classical accept/reject verdict only after completing full-capacity decoding and MAC verification (`mac_inside_encrypt_verify_before_cobs` in `lean/ITB/SeedIsolation.lean` and `desync_preserves_verify_before_cobs` in `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`). Any offline quantum search over candidate keys must therefore execute in the Q1 model by implementing the complete reversible ITB decoding and PRF cascade circuit internally at `O(P)` gate cost per evaluation.

## 2. Quantum Walks on Johnson Graphs and Combinadic State Spaces

A central question in post-quantum symmetric cryptanalysis is whether specialized quantum algorithms for discrete structures and combinatorics can bypass the Rank Barrier faster than generic Grover search.

### 2.1 Johnson Graph Formulation of the Rank Barrier

For each payload chunk, the Rank Barrier partitions `n` bits into three pairwise-disjoint balanced lanes `(m_0, m_1, m_2)` of weight `k = n / 3` (`m_0 ∪ m_1 ∪ m_2 = 2^n − 1`, `popcount(m_j) = k`, [Proof 11](PROOFS.md#proof-11-48-bit-rank-barrier-mask-space-interlocked-barrier)):

- In default 48-bit mode (`Interlock = "48"`, `n = 48`, `k = 16`), `m_0` is drawn from the `A = C(48, 16) = 2,254,848,913,647` (`log₂ ≈ 41.04`) constant-weight 48-bit words of Hamming weight 16, and `m_1` is drawn from the `B = C(32, 16) = 601,080,390` (`log₂ ≈ 29.16`) constant-weight words of weight 16 on the remaining 32 positions (`m_2` is the complement). The per-chunk state space has cardinality:

```
|Ω_chunk| = A · B = 1,355,345,464,406,015,082,330  ≈  2^70.20
```

- In wide 168-bit mode (`Interlock = "168"`, `n = 168`, `k = 56`, for 512-bit PRF-grade primitives), `m_0` is drawn from `A₁₆₈ = C(168, 56) = 1,799,363,822,299,435,101,766,134,108,620,363,111,079,750,360` (`log₂ ≈ 150.33`) and `m_1` from `B₁₆₈ = C(112, 56) = 390,590,044,887,157,789,360,330,532,465,784` (`log₂ ≈ 108.27`), giving:

```
|Ω_chunk,168| = A₁₆₈ · B₁₆₈ = 702,813,596,120,264,168,420,100,264,216,585,729,858,529,081,007,646,807,547,722,792,977,161,961,682,240  ≈  2^258.60
```

In algebraic combinatorics, the set of `C(n, k)` binary vectors of length `n` and Hamming weight `k`, with edges connecting vectors that differ by a single transposition of a 0 and a 1 (Hamming distance `|u △ v| = 2`), forms the **Johnson graph `J(n, k)`**. The Rank Barrier's per-chunk mask space is the Cartesian product of two Johnson graphs:

```
Ω_chunk     ≅ J(48, 16) × J(32, 16)
Ω_chunk,168 ≅ J(168, 56) × J(112, 56)
```

In quantum computing, constant-weight superpositions over `J(n, k)` are **Dicke states** `|D_k^n⟩ = C(n, k)^(-1/2) ∑_(|x|=k) |x⟩`, which can be prepared in `O(n · k)` quantum gates and `O(n)` depth (or `O(n · k)` depth via sequential reversible combinadic unranking circuits). Furthermore, **quantum walks on Johnson graphs** — developed by Ambainis (2004/2007) for element distinctness, Szegedy (2004) for Markov-chain quantization, and Magniez, Nayak, Roland, and Santha (2011, the MNRS framework) — are the primary engine behind quantum Information Set Decoding (ISD) algorithms in code-based cryptography (Bernstein 2010; Kachigar and Tillich 2017; Kirshanova 2018). Because the spectral gap `δ` of `J(n, k)` scales as `Ω(1 / k)`, an MNRS quantum walk can transition between neighboring vertices of `J(n, k)` in `O(1)` update cost while achieving a quadratic speedup `O(1 / √(ε · δ))` over the fraction `ε` of marked vertices.

### 2.2 Four Structural Barriers Against Quantum Walks on `J(n, k)`

Despite the exact correspondence between `Ω_chunk` and `J(n, k) × J(n - k, k)`, quantum walk algorithms cannot achieve a sub-Grover shortcut against the Rank Barrier for four independent reasons:

1. **Absence of a local syndrome equation (`H e^T = s^T`) and Desynchronized Interlocked Barrier.** Quantum walk ISD in code-based cryptography succeeds because the unknown constant-weight error vector `e ∈ J(n, k)` satisfies a public parity-check equation `H e^T = s^T`, allowing the walk to maintain a local linear syndrome register and update a single column per Johnson-graph step. In ITB, the three lane outputs `(lane_0, lane_1, lane_2)` produced by `PEXT` / `BEXT` are never exposed on the wire ([SCIENCE.md § 1.2.1](SCIENCE.md#121-desynchronized-interlocked-barrier), `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`): they are first permuted across `M` chunk slots by **Layer II** (`0x06`, `laneSlotShift`) with guaranteed distinct cyclic shifts `β_0 ≠ β_1 ⟹ μ_0(j) ≠ μ_1(j)` (`desync_beta0_ne_beta1`, `desync_no_fixed_slot_pair`), multiplying single-chunk cross-lane slot matching by `M^3` (`|Ω_chunk| · M^3 ≈ 2^96.91`–`2^101.53` in `"48"` and `≈ 2^279.89`–`2^284.51` in `"168"` across the **Mode 2**, **Mode 3**, and **Mode 1** floors, with `≈ 2^99.54` and `≈ 2^282.52` on **Mode 3**; or `≈ 2^128.91`–`2^133.53` and `≈ 2^311.89`–`2^316.51` with the 4-byte fold `v`, with `≈ 2^131.54` and `≈ 2^314.52` on **Mode 3**; `interlock_desync_bounds_mode2_floor`, `mode3_combined_rank_barrier_exponents_1024`, `interlock_desync_bounds_mode1_floor`). They are then prepended with the hidden interlock-nonce fragments, COBS-encoded with run-length pointers, split across 7-bit channel boundaries (`gcd(7, 8) = 1`), routed along **Layer I**'s dynamic `K_r`-sub-region trajectory (`0x05`, `subregionAddr`, with 3-region pixel trajectory space `(2^7 · 8! · 18^8)^3 ≈ 2^166.98` at `K_r = 8` on the **Mode 2** floor, `≈ 2^187.16` under **Mode 3**'s Gauss-Posit chord schedule at `K_r = 8`, and `(4^7 · 8! · 51^8)^3 ≈ 2^224.04` at `K_r = 8` on the **Mode 1** floor; `subregion_trajectory_bounds_mode2_floor`, `mode3_three_region_joint_layer1_floor_1024`, `subregion_trajectory_bounds_mode1_floor`), rotated by `dataRotation` and XOR-masked by `xorMask` (`dataSeed_1..3`), and interleaved with CSPRNG noise bits (`noiseSeed`). Without already knowing `noiseSeed`, `dataSeed_1..3`, and `startSeed_1..3` (and `lockSeed` for **Layer II**), a quantum walk on `J(48, 16) × J(32, 16)` or `J(168, 56) × J(112, 56)` has no vertex-checking oracle `O_χ` to evaluate whether a candidate mask triple is valid.

2. **Exponential crib multiplicity per chunk (`≈ 2^22.2` and `≈ 2^90.6`).** Even under a hypothetical maximum-peek regime where an attacker is granted the exact `n`-bit plaintext chunk and the exact three `k`-bit lane projections after stripping the Pixel Barrier, the map from mask triples to lane bit values is heavily many-to-one. An `n`-bit known-plaintext chunk supplies at most `n` bits of boolean constraint (`2^n` possible `n`-bit assignments, and at most `C(n, w) < 2^n` on a constant-weight shell of Hamming weight `w`), leaving at least `|Ω_chunk| / 2^n` compatible mask triples in `Ω_chunk`:
   - **48-bit mode (`Interlock = "48"`):** `|Ω_chunk| / 2^48 ≈ 2^(70.20 − 48) = 2^22.2` compatible mask triples per 6-byte chunk ([Proof 11](PROOFS.md#proof-11-48-bit-rank-barrier-mask-space-interlocked-barrier)).
   - **168-bit mode (`Interlock = "168"`):** `|Ω_chunk,168| / 2^168 ≈ 2^(258.60 − 168) = 2^90.6` compatible mask triples per 21-byte chunk ([Proof 11](PROOFS.md#proof-11-48-bit-rank-barrier-mask-space-interlocked-barrier)).

   A quantum walk on `J(n, k)` with `M ≈ 2^22.2` (or `M ≈ 2^90.6`) marked vertices converges in `O(√(|Ω_chunk| / M))` steps to a uniform superposition over all `M` compatible masks, collapsing upon measurement to a random compatible mask that carries zero assurance of matching the true mask derived from `lockSeed`.

3. **Many-to-one PRF preimage cone and unobservable reduction granularity.** Even if the true mask triple `(m_0, m_1, m_2)` of a single chunk were uniquely identified, recovering `lockSeed` requires inverting the two-step reduction `q = ⌊R / B⌋`, `idx1 = R mod B`, `idx0 = q mod A` and the upstream `ChainHash` cascade. By [Proof 11](PROOFS.md#proof-11-48-bit-rank-barrier-mask-space-interlocked-barrier) (`twoStepUnrank_witness`, `twoStepWitness_injective`, `Preimages48_eq`, and `Preimages168_eq` in `lean/ITB/RankBarrier.lean`), every mask triple in `[0, A) × [0, B)` has at least:
   - `⌊2^128 / (A · B)⌋ = 251,066,887,267,792,206 ≈ 2^57.80` distinct 128-bit PRF output preimages in 48-bit mode;
   - `⌊2^512 / (A₁₆₈ · B₁₆₈)⌋ = 19,077,331,463,075,847,632,709,418,264,551,801,233,111,994,340,630,406,080,247,337,248,653,165,811,287 ≈ 2^253.40` distinct 512-bit PRF output preimages in 168-bit mode.

   The deterministic two-step reduction avoids rejection sampling to preserve strict constant-time execution (verified for both the 48-bit reciprocal schedule `splitRank48Recip` (`divB` and `modA`) via `divB48_single_correction_exact` and `modA48_single_correction_exact`, and the 168-bit multi-limb Barrett-reciprocal schedule `splitRank168` (`divmodStepB168` and `modStepA168`) via `divmodStepB168_error_envelope`, `modStepA168_error_envelope`, and `single_correction_exact` in `lean/ITB/SplitRank.lean`). This reduction introduces a per-chunk relative preimage granularity of `≈ 2^-57.8` in 48-bit mode (`≈ 2^-34.4` accumulated over a maximum 64 MiB message of `11,184,812 ≈ 2^23.42` chunks) and `≈ 2^-253.4` in 168-bit mode (`≈ 2^-231.8` accumulated over `3,195,661 ≈ 2^21.61` chunks). Classical statistical distinguishing requires `1 / ε² ≈ 2^115.6` chunks (~2^92 maximum-size messages) in 48-bit mode and `≈ 2^506.8` chunks (~2^485 maximum-size messages, exceeding the ~2^306 Landauer bound) in 168-bit mode. Even with quantum amplitude estimation (Brassard, Høyer, Mosca, and Tapp 2002), which estimates a bias `ε` in `O(1 / ε)` coherent oracle queries, the biased event is an internal property of one-way PRF outputs buried beneath the Pixel Barrier and cannot be queried coherently from ciphertext observations.

4. **Per-chunk PRF independence destroys Johnson-graph locality across chunks.** In a multi-chunk message of `C` chunks, the mask of chunk `groupIdx` is generated from an independent PRF evaluation `ChainHash([0x03 ‖ LE64(groupIdx) ‖ v], lockComps)`. Moving one edge on the Johnson graph `J(n, k)` in chunk `groupIdx` does not correspond to a small step in seed space, and constrains zero bits of chunk `groupIdx + 1`. Consequently, any search over the mask sequence across `C` chunks faces the product space `(A · B)^C ≈ 2^(70.20 × C)` in 48-bit mode and `(A₁₆₈ · B₁₆₈)^C ≈ 2^(258.60 × C)` in 168-bit mode — which already exceeds `2^512` at `C = 2` chunks (42 B, `2^517.2 > 2^512`), `2^1024` at `C = 4` chunks (84 B, `2^1034.4 > 2^1024`), and `2^2048` at `C = 8` chunks (168 B, `2^2068.8 > 2^2048`, `Omega168_two_chunks_dominates_512`, `Omega168_four_chunks_dominates_1024`, and `Omega168_eight_chunks_dominates_2048` in `lean/ITB/RankBarrier.lean`).

## 3. Non-Abelian Hidden Subgroup Problem and Semidirect Channel Actions

Beyond Grover search and quantum walks, exponential and subexponential quantum speedups (Shor, Simon, Kuperberg, Regev) exploit algebraic periodicity via the **Hidden Subgroup Problem (HSP)** or the **Hidden Shift Problem**. Both barriers of ITB induce non-abelian group actions whose structure prevents quantum Fourier sampling and collimation sieves.

### 3.1 Non-Abelian HSP over the Symmetric Group `S_48` and `S_168` (Rank Barrier)

Each balanced 3-way partition of an `n`-bit chunk into three ordered lanes of size `k = n / 3` corresponds to a left coset of the Young subgroup `H = S_k × S_k × S_k` inside the full symmetric group `S_n` (`S_48 / (S_16)³` of size `48! / (16!)³ = C(48, 16) · C(32, 16) ≈ 2^70.20` in 48-bit mode, and `S_168 / (S_56)³` of size `168! / (56!)³ = C(168, 56) · C(112, 56) ≈ 2^258.60` in 168-bit mode).

Recovering a hidden permutation or Young-subgroup coset from quantum queries is an instance of the **Non-Abelian Hidden Subgroup Problem over the symmetric group `S_n`**, one of the most thoroughly studied hard barriers in quantum complexity theory:

- **Failure of Weak and Strong Quantum Fourier Sampling on `S_n`.** Hallgren, Russell, and Ta-Shma (2000/2003) and Grigni, Schulman, Vazirani, and Vazirani (2001/2004) established that standard (weak) Fourier sampling over non-abelian groups with large-dimensional irreducible representations yields exponentially small distinguishing bias on `S_n`. Subsequently, **Moore, Russell, and Schulman (2005/2008)** proved an unconditional **exponential lower bound (`2^Ω(n)`)** demonstrating that **strong Fourier sampling** (projective measurements in arbitrary bases over the irreducible representations of `S_n` on single coset registers) requires exponentially many quantum queries, and **Hallgren, Moore, Rötteler, Russell, and Sen (2006/2010)** proved that even entangled measurements across `k` registers yield exponentially small distinguishing advantage unless `k = Ω(n log n)` coset registers are measured jointly. Alagic and Russell (2017) further showed that symmetric-group operations resist quantum period-finding when composed with independent masking.
- **Per-chunk `ChainHash` re-keying and Layer II cross-lane slot desynchronization (`0x06`) eliminate coset periodicity.** Even if a hypothetical quantum circuit could bypass the Moore–Russell–Schulman and Hallgren–Moore–Rötteler–Russell–Sen barriers on a fixed permutation in `S_48` or `S_168`, the Rank Barrier never applies a fixed permutation twice. Every chunk `groupIdx` within a message draws an independent rank from `ChainHash([0x03 ‖ LE64(groupIdx) ‖ v], lockComps)`, every message draws a fresh `interlock_nonce` `N_il` under domain tag `0x04`, and **Layer II** composes the intra-chunk Young cosets with three independent inter-chunk slot permutations `Σ_0, Σ_1, Σ_2 ∈ S_M` (`β_0 ≠ β_1` under domain tag `0x06`, `desync_beta0_ne_beta1`, `desync_no_fixed_slot_pair` in `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`). No periodic coset invariant exists across chunks or across messages for a quantum Fourier transform to constructively interfere upon.

### 3.2 Dihedral / Semidirect Hidden Shift vs. Pixel Barrier (`GF(2)^7 ⋊ Z_7`)

In the Pixel Barrier, each 8-byte pixel `p` in region `i ∈ {1, 2, 3}` derives a 64-bit digest `dataHash` from `ChainHash([LE32(p) ‖ N_m], dataSeed_i)` (`counter ‖ N_m`, [SCIENCE.md § 2.3](SCIENCE.md#23-8-seed-isolation-theorems-3-3a)), extracting a shared 7-state cyclic rotation `dataRotation = dataHash mod 7 ∈ Z_7` (3 config bits) and a 56-bit mask `xorMask = dataHash >> 3` (8 channels × 7 bits per channel). On each 7-bit channel payload `d ∈ GF(2)^7`, the channel encoding prior to noise insertion is:

```
d' = rotate7(d ⊕ m, r)   where m ∈ GF(2)^7, r ∈ Z_7
```

Because cyclic bit rotation `rotate7(·, r)` is a group automorphism of `(GF(2)^7, ⊕)`, the pairs `(m, r)` generate the **non-abelian semidirect product group `G_ch = GF(2)^7 ⋊ Z_7`** of order `2^7 × 7 = 896` per channel (and `G_px = GF(2)^56 ⋊ Z_7` of order `7 × 2^56` across all 8 channels of a pixel sharing `r`, with every `(r, mask56)` pair realized across 36 or 37 of the 256 non-mask bit assignments by `dataHash_rot_mask_realizable` in `lean/ITB/PixelBarrier.lean`). Semidirect products of the form `A ⋊ Z_m` generalize the dihedral group `D_N = Z_N ⋊ Z_2`, for which **Kuperberg (2005/2013)** and **Regev (2004)** developed subexponential `2^O(√(log N))` and `2^O(√(log N log log N))` quantum sieves for the **Hidden Shift Problem** (also used in quantum cryptanalysis of shift-based constructions via Offline-Simon, Bonnetain et al. 2019).

Four independent architectural properties block Kuperberg/Regev hidden-shift sieves, Simon's algorithm (`f(x) = f(x ⊕ s)`), Offline-Simon, and Brassard–Høyer–Tapp (BHT) quantum claw-finding against the Pixel Barrier:

1. **Exact 7-to-1 `xorMask` absorption (Proof 2).** Because `rotate7` is linear over `GF(2)^7` (`rotate7(d ⊕ m, r) = rotate7(d, r) ⊕ rotate7(m, r)`), the rotation `r ∈ Z_7` does not create a distinct orbital coset when `m` is unknown and drawn uniformly. By [Proof 2](PROOFS.md#proof-2-per-bit-xor-kpa-resistance) (machine-checked in `lean/ITB/PixelBarrier.lean` as `candidateMask_consistent`, `candidateMask_unique`, `proof2_exists_unique_mask`, and `proof2_candidate_counts`), for every observed wire byte `v ∈ BitVec 8`, every known 7-bit data vector `d ∈ BitVec 7`, every noise position `np ∈ Fin 8`, and **every** rotation `r ∈ Fin 7`, there exists a unique compatible mask:

   ```
   candidateMask(v, d, np, r) = unrotate7(extractBits(v, np), r) ⊕ d
   ```

   producing `v` bit-for-bit. All 7 rotations in `Z_7` (and all `8 × 7 = 56` `(np, r)` pairs without CCA) are mathematically equiprobable and observationally identical at the pixel boundary.

2. **Per-pixel and per-message shift non-stationarity.** Kuperberg's sieve and Simon's algorithm require coherent quantum queries to a function hiding a **fixed** global shift `s` across `Ω(log N)` or `2^O(√(log N))` states. In ITB, `(m, r)` changes at every pixel `p` via the keyed PRF `ChainHash` over `LE32(p)` and changes across every message via the fresh 512-bit `main_nonce` `N_m`. No two pixels or messages share a hidden shift unless the underlying PRF is already inverted.

3. **Phase decoherence by per-byte CSPRNG noise (Proof 1).** Following the `GF(2)^7 ⋊ Z_7` channel transform, `insertBit` injects 1 true CSPRNG bit from `internal/drbg` at `noisePos ∈ Z_8`. In the quantum query model, an oracle that mixes each output byte with fresh, independent entropy entangles the attacker's query register with an unobserved environment register (`internal/drbg`), tracing out phase correlations and destroying the destructive interference required by Simon's and Kuperberg's Quantum Fourier Transforms.

4. **Cross-ring algebraic incompatibility across the full pipeline.** Between plaintext entry and wire output, data traverses seven algebraically incompatible stages and non-linear maps:
   - **PRF cascade (`ChainHash`):** Keyed non-linear sponge/compression/ARX/AES rounds (`h_i = H(data, s_i ⊕ h_(i-1))`, [Proof 3b](PROOFS.md#proof-3b-chainhash-full-component-utilization)).
   - **Multi-limb Euclidean division and modulo (`Z` mod `B`, mod `A`):** Two-step reduction `(⌊R / B⌋ mod A, R mod B)` over coprime-free binomial moduli with `gcd(A, B) = 66,861` (`Interlock = "48"`) and `gcd(A₁₆₈, B₁₆₈) = 10,078,335,949,946,663,736` (`Interlock = "168"`, [Proof 12](PROOFS.md#proof-12-gcda-b-anti-collapse-trap)).
   - **Combinadic threshold subtraction (`C(p, k)`):** Non-linear order-statistic comparisons over Pascal's triangle.
   - **Position-compacting bit projection (`PEXT` / `BEXT`) and Layer II cross-lane slot desynchronization (`0x06`):** Data-dependent wire routing where the destination index of bit `i` depends on `popcount(mask & ((1 << i) - 1))`, followed by three independent chunk-slot permutations `Σ_0, Σ_1, Σ_2` derived via the non-affine 128-bit ARX bijection `fullWidthFold(hLo, hHi)` (`fullWidthFold_bijection`, `fullWidthFold_preserves_prf_cardinality`, `fullWidthFold_non_affine_gf2`, `fullWidthFold_fnv1a_dead_zone_coverage` in `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`) with guaranteed distinct shifts `β_0 ≠ β_1` (`desync_beta0_ne_beta1`, `desync_no_fixed_slot_pair` in `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`).
   - **COBS run-length framing (`[1, 255]`):** Zero-elimination framing with variable-distance pointer bytes (`0x00` delimiter followed by guaranteed `≥ 7 × (2s + 1)` DRBG fill bytes, [Proof 10](PROOFS.md#proof-10-guaranteed-drbg-residue-no-perfect-fill)).
   - **Coprime byte-to-channel splitting (`gcd(7, 8) = 1`) and Layer I sub-region trajectory (`0x05`):** Every 8-bit lane byte straddles two 7-bit pixel channels with zero interior alignment (`byte_channel_no_interior_alignment` and `every_byte_spans_two_adjacent_channels` in `lean/ITB/Thresholds.lean`), embedded across `K_r` jittered, Fisher-Yates-permuted sub-regions (or **Mode 3** Gauss-circle chords accumulated in `posit64(es)` / `quire512` with non-associative RNE rounding, `buildSubregionSchedulePosit` and `positMul_positAdd_nonassociative_witness` in `lean/ITB/Posit.lean`) with independent local starting offsets `s_{r,k} ∈ [0, M_{r,k})` (`subregionStep_left_inv`, `subregionAddr_injective` in `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`).
   - **Semidirect channel action (`GF(2)^7 ⋊ Z_7`) + noise insertion (`BitVec 7 × Bool → BitVec 8`):** Per-pixel cyclic rotation, XOR masking, and 1-of-8 bit injection.

   No group homomorphism commutes across even two consecutive stages of this pipeline.

## 4. Oracle-Free Amplitude Amplification and Ambiguity Dominance

Grover's algorithm and its generalization, **Quantum Amplitude Amplification** (Brassard, Høyer, Mosca, and Tapp 2002), search an unstructured domain of `N` candidates by repeatedly applying the Grover iterate `G = (2|ψ⟩⟨ψ| − I) O_χ`, where `|ψ⟩ = N^(-1/2) ∑_x |x⟩` is the uniform superposition and `O_χ |x⟩ = (-1)^χ(x) |x⟩` flips the phase of states satisfying a boolean verification predicate `χ(x) ∈ {0, 1}`.

Let `M = |{x : χ(x) = 1}|` denote the number of candidates that satisfy the predicate `χ`. In the 2-dimensional subspace spanned by the unmarked superposition `|α⟩` and the marked superposition `|β⟩`, each application of `G` rotates the state vector by angle `θ`, where:

```
sin²(θ / 2) = M / N
```

After `t` iterations, the probability of measuring a marked state is `sin²((2t + 1) θ / 2)`. This geometric identity governs the exact interaction between quantum amplitude amplification and ITB's **Ambiguity Dominance** ([Proof 9](PROOFS.md#proof-9-ambiguity-dominance-threshold)):

### 4.1 The Oracle-Free and Single-Domain Regimes (`M / N = 1`: Zero Amplitude Concentration)

By [Proof 9](PROOFS.md#proof-9-ambiguity-dominance-threshold) (machine-checked for all three key widths in `lean/ITB/Thresholds.lean` via `threshold_512_cca_above`, `threshold_512_nocca_above`, `threshold_1024_cca_above`, `threshold_1024_nocca_above`, `threshold_2048_cca_above`, and `threshold_2048_nocca_above`), the container sizing logic enforces `P ≥ MinPixels = ⌈k / log₂ 7⌉` (183, 365, 730 pixels for `k = 512, 1024, 2048` under CCA; vs. `⌈k / log₂ 56⌉ = 89, 177, 353` pixels without CCA). At the 1024-bit key floor, the number of observation-consistent configurations across the container strictly exceeds the `2^1024` key space in every sizing mode:

- **Mode 1 (`35 × 35`, `P = 1225`):** Noise barrier `2^(8P) = 2^9800`; encoding ambiguity `56^P ≈ 2^7114` (without CCA) and `7^P ≈ 2^3439` (under CCA); configuration space `2^(62P) = 2^75950`.
- **Mode 3 (`28 × 28`, `P = 784`):** Noise barrier `2^(8P) = 2^6272`; encoding ambiguity `56^P ≈ 2^4553` (without CCA) and `7^P ≈ 2^2201` (under CCA); configuration space `2^(62P) = 2^48608`.
- **Mode 2 (`21 × 21`, `P = 441`):** Noise barrier `2^(8P) = 2^3528`; encoding ambiguity `56^P ≈ 2^2561` (without CCA) and `7^P ≈ 2^1238` (under CCA); configuration space `2^(62P) = 2^27342`.
- **Theoretical single-region floor (`20 × 20`, `P = 400`):** Noise barrier `2^(8P) = 2^3200`; encoding ambiguity `56^P ≈ 2^2323` (without CCA) and `7^P ≈ 2^1123` (under CCA); configuration space `2^(62P) = 2^24800`.

This yields `M = N` (`sin²(θ / 2) = 1`, `θ = π`) across two distinct search domains:

1. **Ciphertext-only observation (COA) over the full seed space (Proof 1 and Proof 8).** In **Core ITB (No MAC)** or in **MAC + Silent Drop without insider knowledge of the internal MAC format**, when the plaintext `M_pt` is unknown, every candidate seed tuple in the key space decodes `C` to a valid byte stream ([Proof 8](PROOFS.md#proof-8-oracle-free-deniability), `proof8_deniability_channel_surjection` in `lean/ITB/SeedIsolation.lean`) and admits a valid pre-embedding container state ([Proof 1](PROOFS.md#proof-1-information-theoretic-barrier)).
2. **Full KPA over isolated single seeds or unkeyed pixel configurations (Proof 2, Proof 3, and Proof 9).** Even under Full KPA where the complete `(M_pt, C)` pair is known, every unkeyed pixel configuration in `(Fin 8 × Fin 7)^P` (`56^P > 2^k` without CCA, `7^P > 2^k` under CCA) admits a unique compatible `xorMask` along any **Layer I** sub-region trajectory `Π_r` ([Proof 2](PROOFS.md#proof-2-per-bit-xor-kpa-resistance), `desync_preserves_proof2_unique_mask`), and any candidate `noiseSeed` or `startSeed_i` tested in isolation without `dataSeed_i` and `lockSeed` is consistent with `C` ([Proof 3](PROOFS.md#proof-3-8-seed-isolation)).

In both cases, the consistency predicate `χ(x)` evaluates to `1` on the entire domain (`M = N`):

```
M = N   ⇒   M / N = 1   ⇒   sin²(θ / 2) = 1   (θ = π)
```

When `θ = π`, the oracle `O_χ = -I` flips the phase of every basis state equally, and the Grover operator `G = (2|ψ⟩⟨ψ| − I)(-I) = I − 2|ψ⟩⟨ψ|` acts on the initial uniform superposition as `G|ψ⟩ = -|ψ⟩` (a trivial global phase of `-1`). Regardless of how many quantum iterations `t` are executed, the measurement distribution remains flat (`1 / N`), yielding **zero amplitude concentration** under COA and preventing divide-and-conquer single-seed Grover shortcuts under Full KPA.

### 4.2 The Full-Container Verification Regime (`M ≈ 1`: BBBV Lower Bound)

When a decisive verification predicate over the full seed tuple is available — because the attacker possesses a known plaintext `M_pt` under Full KPA, because the deployment operates in MAC + Reveal, or because the attacker in MAC + Silent Drop knows the internal MAC structure and evaluates `MAC(payload) == tag` inside the quantum circuit — the full-container check filters out false candidate seed tuples, reducing the marked set to `M ≈ 1`.

In this regime, the **Bennett–Bernstein–Brassard–Vazirani (BBBV 1997) theorem** proves that because the per-key verification predicate depends on the output of a pseudorandom cascade (`ChainHash`), no quantum circuit can identify the marked seed tuple in fewer than `Ω(√N)` oracle calls. Furthermore, because `noiseSeed` and `dataSeed` must be searched jointly without CCA ([Proof 3](PROOFS.md#proof-3-8-seed-isolation)) and `authDecode` verifies the MAC over the entire container capacity before inspecting COBS framing ([PROOFS.md § MAC-Inside-Encrypt Composition](PROOFS.md#mac-inside-encrypt-composition)), the quantum oracle cannot short-circuit evaluation on a single seed, pixel, or chunk: every Grover iteration must execute the full `O(P)` reversible container decryption and unranking pipeline, recovering the conservative baseline of § 1.1 (`√P × 2^keyBits` for Core ITB Full KPA and MAC + Silent Drop with insider MAC check, and `√P × 2^(keyBits/2)` for MAC + Reveal).

## 5. Classical Combinatorial Algorithms (Knuth TAOCP Volume 4A / 4B)

Because the Rank Barrier and Pixel Barrier are built from discrete mathematics — exact balanced 3-way partitions, combinadic unranking over Pascal's triangle, `PEXT`/`PDEP` bit routing, and coprime 7-to-8 channel packing — it is natural to ask whether the classical combinatorial search and compression algorithms codified in Donald E. Knuth's **The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 4A (Combinatorial Algorithms, Part 1)** and **Volume 4B (Combinatorial Algorithms, Part 2)** can decompose or prune the barrier spaces without the seeds.

Below is the structural analysis of the four principal paradigms from TAOCP Volume 4A/4B against the Interlocked Barrier.

### 5.1 Dancing Links (DLX / Algorithm X and Algorithm M for Exact Cover, TAOCP Vol. 4B § 7.2.2.1)

**Target mapping.** Partitioning a 48-bit chunk into three pairwise-disjoint 16-bit lanes `(m_0, m_1, m_2)` satisfying `m_0 ∪ m_1 ∪ m_2 = 2^48 − 1` and `popcount(m_j) = 16` (or a 168-bit chunk into three 56-bit lanes in `Interlock = "168"`) is an exact instance of the **Exact Cover** problem with multiplicity constraints (1 per bit position and 16 or 56 per lane), for which Knuth's **Algorithm X** and **Algorithm M** (**Dancing Links / DLX**, TAOCP Vol. 4B § 7.2.2.1) are the canonical solvers.

**Why DLX degenerates to exhaustive search:**
- **Zero local exclusion constraints.** DLX achieves high throughput on combinatorial puzzles (such as polyomino tiling, N-Queens, or Sudoku) because the incidence matrix is sparse and locally over-constrained: covering one column immediately conflicts with and deletes a large fraction of remaining candidate rows, pruning dead branches at shallow depth.
- **Total syntactic validity of `Ω_chunk`.** In the Rank Barrier, **every single one** of the `|Ω_chunk| = C(48, 16) · C(32, 16) = 1,355,345,464,406,015,082,330 ≈ 2^70.20` balanced partitions in 48-bit mode — and every single one of the `|Ω_chunk,168| = C(168, 56) · C(112, 56) ≈ 2^258.60` balanced partitions in 168-bit mode — is syntactically valid and reachable with near-uniform probability (`⌊2^128 / (A · B)⌋ ≈ 2^57.80` or `⌊2^512 / (A₁₆₈ · B₁₆₈)⌋ ≈ 2^253.40` preimages per triple, [Proof 11](PROOFS.md#proof-11-48-bit-rank-barrier-mask-space-interlocked-barrier)).
- **No partial-branch contradiction.** Selecting bit `i` for lane 0 imposes no local constraint other than the global weight budget `popcount(m_0) = 16` (or 56). Furthermore, **Layer II** (`0x06`, `laneSlotShift`, `desync_beta0_ne_beta1`) permutes the `M` chunk slots across `(p0, p1, p2)` with `Σ_0(j) ≠ Σ_1(j)`, multiplying the single-chunk cross-lane slot-matching space by `M^3`, while **Layer I** (`0x05`, `subregionAddr`) routes the COBS-framed lane bytes along secret sub-region trajectories `Π_r`. Even under Full KPA where the chunk's input bits are known, `≈ 2^22.2` (48-bit) or `≈ 2^90.6` (168-bit) exact covers remain consistent with any candidate lane output. Because no branch can be refuted until all `C` chunks are slot-aligned, unranked, reassembled, COBS-decoded, and verified against a full-container MAC, DLX performs zero constraint propagation and degenerates into unpruned enumeration of `(A · B)^C`.

### 5.2 Binary and Zero-Suppressed Decision Diagrams (BDDs / ZDDs, TAOCP Vol. 4A § 7.1.4)

**Target mapping.** Knuth's **Binary Decision Diagrams (BDDs)** and Minato/Knuth **Zero-Suppressed Decision Diagrams (ZDDs)** represent massive families of combinations (such as subsets of `J(n, k)` or solutions to boolean constraints) as compressed directed acyclic graphs by merging isomorphic subproblems across a fixed variable ordering.

**Why BDD/ZDD compilation explodes exponentially:**
1. **PRF cascade and multi-limb division destroy subtree isomorphism.** A standalone ZDD can represent the static set of all `C(48, 16) · C(32, 16)` or `C(168, 56) · C(112, 56)` balanced partitions compactly by counting running popcounts `(c_0, c_1, c_2)`. However, in ITB the active partition for chunk `groupIdx` is not a free combinatorial variable; it is a deterministic function of `ChainHash([0x03 ‖ LE64(groupIdx) ‖ v], lockComps)` reduced through the two-step Euclidean division `q = ⌊R / B⌋`, `idx1 = R mod B`, `idx0 = q mod A` (`splitRank48Recip` via `divB` / `modA` in 48-bit mode and `splitRank168` via `divmodStepB168` / `modStepA168` in 168-bit mode, [Proof 12](PROOFS.md#proof-12-gcda-b-anti-collapse-trap), avoiding the `gcd(A, B) = 66,861` and `gcd(A₁₆₈, B₁₆₈) = 10,078,335,949,946,663,736` collapse of naive double-modulo reduction). Compiling integer multiplication/division and a multi-round cryptographic PRF into an ordered BDD or ZDD provably induces **exponential width (`2^Ω(w)`)** under any variable ordering due to high communication complexity across the middle cut.
2. **Dynamic bit routing via `PEXT` / `BEXT` and Layer I/II desynchronized permutations.** In the Rank Barrier, the lane destination of input bit `i` is not a fixed wire; it equals `popcount(m_r & ((1 << i) − 1))`, which depends on the sum of all mask bits at positions `< i`, and is then relocated across chunk slots by **Layer II** (`0x06`, `Σ_0, Σ_1, Σ_2`) and across physical container pixels by **Layer I** (`0x05`, `Π_r`). When the mask and trajectory schedules are symbolic (unknown `lockSeed` and `startSeed_i`), two ZDD paths with different prefix assignments route the same plaintext bit `i` to different chunk slots, different COBS blocks, and different Pixel Barrier sub-regions and channels, preventing ZDD node sharing across the boundary between the Rank Barrier and the Pixel Barrier.

### 5.3 Backtracking and Branch-and-Bound with Early Cutoff (TAOCP Vol. 4B §§ 7.2.2, 7.2.2.2)

**Target mapping.** Classical backtracking (**Algorithm B** and **Algorithm W**, TAOCP Vol. 4B § 7.2.2) and conflict-driven clause learning in SAT/SMT solvers (**Algorithm D** and **Algorithm C**, TAOCP Vol. 4B § 7.2.2.2) search a state tree of depth `n` by evaluating a **partial-prefix predicate** `P_d(x_1, ..., x_d)` at depth `d ≪ n`, cutting off the entire `2^(n - d)` subtree whenever a local inconsistency is detected.

**Why early cutoff is architecturally foreclosed in ITB:**
1. **Universal local consistency at every pixel (Proof 1 and Proof 2).** At the Pixel Barrier, every wire byte contains 1 noise bit and 7 data bits. By [Proof 2](PROOFS.md#proof-2-per-bit-xor-kpa-resistance) (`proof2_exists_unique_mask` and `proof2_candidate_counts` in `lean/ITB/PixelBarrier.lean`), for any candidate data byte and any observed wire byte `v`, all 56 local `(noisePos, dataRotation)` configurations (or all 7 `dataRotation` values under CCA) admit a unique compatible 7-bit `xorMask`. Consequently, a partial assignment of pixel configurations never triggers a local bit contradiction at depth `d = 1, 2, ...`; every branch remains locally consistent.
2. **Coprime byte-splitting (`gcd(7, 8) = 1`).** Because each pixel channel carries 7 data bits while COBS and Rank Barrier lanes operate on 8-bit bytes, `gcd(7, 8) = 1` ensures that no interior byte boundary `8b` (`b ∈ {1, ..., 6}`) coincides with any interior channel boundary `7c` (`c ∈ {1, ..., 7}`, `byte_channel_no_interior_alignment` and `every_byte_spans_two_adjacent_channels` in `lean/ITB/Thresholds.lean`). Every payload byte is split across two adjacent channels, coupling neighboring channel masks and preventing byte-isolated pruning under Partial KPA ([Proof 4a](PROOFS.md#proof-4a-multi-factor-full-kpa-resistance)).
3. **Absence of COBS framing and padding oracles (Proof 10) and Layer I/II trajectory/slot isolation.** In standard block cipher modes (e.g., PKCS#7 padding or structured headers at fixed offsets), a solver can prune branches by checking whether the first decrypted block satisfies formatting constraints. In ITB, all bytes inside the COBS payload take values in `[1, 255]`, the `0x00` delimiter sits at an unrevealed offset shifted by the secret interlock-nonce fragment and routed along the **Layer I** sub-region trajectory `Π_r` (`0x05`, spanning `≈ 2^166.98` 3-region trajectories on the **Mode 2** floor at `K_r = 8`, `≈ 2^187.16` on the **Mode 3** floor, and `≈ 2^224.04` on the **Mode 1** floor), and the remaining container capacity after `0x00` is filled with at least `7 × (2s + 1)` bytes of encrypted CSPRNG-seeded DRBG residue (≥ 287 bytes at **Mode 2** `P = 441`, ≥ 385 bytes at **Mode 3** `P = 784`, ≥ 483 bytes at **Mode 1** `P = 1225`, ≥ 273 bytes at `P = 400`, [Proof 10](PROOFS.md#proof-10-guaranteed-drbg-residue-no-perfect-fill), `drbg_gap_identity` and `drbg_no_perfect_fill` in `lean/ITB/Thresholds.lean`). Every partial decryption looks like valid non-zero COBS bytes or indistinguishable DRBG fill.
4. **Empirical confirmation in the audit suite.** This theoretical absence of early cutoff is empirically confirmed by the FNV-1a lo-lane SAT stress test ([REDTEAM.md § FNV-1a lo-lane SAT — architecturally foreclosed](REDTEAM.md#fnv-1a-lo-lane-sat--architecturally-foreclosed)): even when the cryptographic PRF is replaced by the non-cryptographic FNV-1a hash on all 8 seeds and the solver (Bitwuzla) is granted 5 of the 8 chains plus `startPixel_i` via a lab peek, the Rank Barrier's per-chunk permutation and **Layer I/II** desynchronization (folded through `fullWidthFold`, which breaks lower-triangular T-function locality and covers FNV-1a's upper-half bits `[88..127]`, `fullWidthFold_fnv1a_dead_zone_coverage` in `lean/ITB/Desync.lean`) foreclose anchor formulation, returning UNSAT or false models rejected by cross-validation on 5 / 5 corpus bundles.

### 5.4 Combinatorial Gray Codes and Revolving-Door Generation (Algorithm R, TAOCP Vol. 4A § 7.2.1.3)

**Target mapping.** Knuth's **Revolving-Door Algorithm R** generates all `C(n, k)` combinations in `J(n, k)` as a Hamiltonian path (a combinatorial Gray code) in which consecutive masks `m^(t)` and `m^(t+1)` differ by swapping a single 1 and 0 (`|m^(t) △ m^(t+1)| = 2`). In optimization and heuristic cryptanalysis (such as hill-climbing, simulated annealing, or incremental differential evaluation), Gray-code traversal enables `O(1)` incremental updates per step and relies on Lipschitz continuity — a minimal step in mask space producing a minimal perturbation in the decoded state.

**Why Gray-code traversal induces global avalanche across the barriers:**
1. **Non-local bit-index shift in `PEXT` / `BEXT` and Layer II slot desynchronization.** When Revolving-Door Algorithm R swaps bit position `i` (`1 → 0`) with bit position `j` (`0 → 1`, `i < j`) in mask `m_0`, `PEXT(chunk, m_0)` does not merely flip two output bits: every set bit of `m_0` located at an index `p` strictly between `i` and `j` (`i < p < j`) has its cumulative rank `popcount(m_0 & ((1 << p) − 1))` shifted by `±1`. A single Gray-code step in `J(48, 16)` or `J(168, 56)` can therefore shift up to 16 (or 56) bit positions simultaneously across `lane_0` and `lane_1`, cascading across 2 to 7 bytes of the lane stream and across desynchronized chunk slots `Σ_0, Σ_1, Σ_2` (`0x06`).
2. **COBS pointer chain displacement.** If a single byte perturbation in a lane transitions to or from the delimiter value `0x00` (or alters a COBS block-length pointer byte), the COBS decoder reinterprets the distance to the next zero byte, desynchronizing the framing of subsequent bytes in that region.
3. **Cyclic `rotate7` and `gcd(7, 8) = 1` dispersion.** In the inverse direction (modifying a wire hypothesis and stepping through pixel configurations), changing `dataRotation` by `±1 (mod 7)` cyclically shifts all 7 data bits of all 8 channels (56 bits per pixel), which after `gcd(7, 8) = 1` byte reassembly scatters across 7 consecutive COBS bytes before hitting the inverse `PDEP` / `BDEP` scatter of the Rank Barrier.
4. **PRF preimage discontinuity.** Two masks `m^(t)` and `m^(t+1)` that are adjacent in Revolving-Door Gray-code order have combinadic ranks `idx0^(t)` and `idx0^(t+1)` mapped from unrelated 128-bit or 512-bit `ChainHash` outputs. A local walk in `J(n, k)` is completely disconnected from the topology of the underlying seed space `2^keyBits`.
